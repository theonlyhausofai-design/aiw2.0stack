# Med Spa CRO Patterns

Conversion rate optimization patterns specific to med spa websites. Section order, hero composition, trust stacking, form design, sticky elements, and divergences from generic service business sites.

---

## Optimal Section Order (Top to Bottom)

This is the highest-converting page flow based on top-performing med spa sites.

1. **Hero** (above the fold)
2. **Trust bar** (credentials, affiliations, certifications)
3. **Before/after gallery** (treatment-specific, 3-6 examples)
4. **Services overview** (card grid with treatment categories)
5. **About the providers** (photo, credentials, short bio)
6. **Patient testimonials** (video preferred, text with photo acceptable)
7. **Membership/special offer** (value-first, low commitment)
8. **FAQ** (addresses fears and objections directly)
9. **Final CTA** (booking form or phone)
10. **Footer** (location, hours, contact, map)

**Why this order works:** The hero hooks. The trust bar proves legitimacy before the visitor scrolls. Before/afters appear early because they are the single strongest conversion element. Services come after trust is established. Provider bios reinforce credentials. Testimonials provide social proof. The FAQ handles remaining objections. The final CTA catches anyone still on the fence.

## Hero Composition

**Elements that convert (in priority order):**

1. **Background image or video:** Real treatment room or provider at work. Never stock. Never a generic model. The space itself signals quality and professionalism.

2. **Headline:** Transformation-focused, patient-centered language. 8 words or fewer. "Look Like You, Just Refreshed" outperforms "Welcome to [Practice Name]."

3. **Subheadline:** One sentence. Addresses the top fear or states the top credential. "Board-certified providers. Natural-looking results."

4. **Primary CTA button:** High contrast. Action-specific. "Book Your Free Consultation" beats "Contact Us" by a wide margin.

5. **Secondary CTA:** "See Our Results" or "View Before & Afters." Gives research-phase visitors a path that does not require commitment.

6. **Trust indicators in hero:** Star rating, review count, or a single credential badge (Allergan Diamond, board certification seal). Small, non-intrusive, but visible.

**What kills hero conversion:**
- Stock photography of models
- Rotating carousels (they dilute the message)
- No CTA above the fold
- Practice name as the headline
- Too much text

## Trust Stack Ordering

The sequence matters. Each layer builds on the one before it.

### Layer 1: Credentials (immediate, above fold or just below)
- Board certification badges
- Allergan Diamond/Platinum provider status
- Medical license numbers
- Fellowship or residency details
- Years in practice

### Layer 2: Visual Proof (first scroll)
- Before/after photo gallery
- Treatment-specific organization
- Consistent lighting, angle, and framing
- No filters, no editing beyond cropping
- Patient consent badges or notices visible

### Layer 3: Social Proof (second scroll)
- Google review count and star rating
- RealSelf rating if applicable
- Video testimonials from real patients
- Named testimonials with treatment type and photo
- "As seen in" press logos if applicable

### Layer 4: Process Proof (mid-page)
- Step-by-step consultation process
- What to expect during treatment
- Aftercare instructions
- Follow-up communication promises

### Layer 5: Risk Reversal (near CTA)
- Free consultation offer
- Satisfaction commitments
- Touch-up policies
- Transparent pricing
- No-pressure language

## Form Patterns

### Primary booking form:
- **Fields:** Name, phone, email, treatment interest (dropdown), preferred date (optional)
- **Maximum fields:** 5. Every additional field reduces completion by 10-15%.
- **CTA button text:** "Book My Consultation" (first person possessive outperforms second person)
- **Below button:** "We respond within 2 hours" or similar speed promise

### Secondary capture (lower commitment):
- **Quiz:** "Which treatment is right for you?" 4-6 questions, email gate at results
- **Guide download:** "Download our Botox FAQ" or treatment guide, email gate
- **SMS opt-in:** "Text GLOW to [number] for our new patient special"

### Form placement:
- Hero section (sticky or embedded)
- After before/after gallery (high intent moment)
- After testimonials (social proof builds confidence)
- Footer (catch-all)
- Mobile: sticky bottom bar with "Book Now" that opens a modal form

## Sticky Elements

### Desktop:
- Fixed header with phone number and "Book Now" button
- Header should collapse on scroll to a slim bar
- Phone number click-to-call even on desktop

### Mobile:
- Sticky bottom bar with two buttons: "Call Now" and "Book Online"
- The bar stays fixed during scroll
- Takes up no more than 60px of screen height
- Phone button triggers native dialer
- Book button opens a minimal modal form (name, phone, treatment)

### Sticky chat/text widget:
- Bottom right corner
- "Text us" preferred over generic chat
- Opens SMS on mobile, chat widget on desktop
- Should appear after 5 seconds or 25% scroll depth

## Before/After Gallery Patterns

**What top performers do:**

1. **Organize by treatment.** Separate galleries for Botox, lip filler, cheek filler, body contouring, laser treatments. Patients search by treatment, not by date.

2. **Consistent photography standards.** Same lighting, same angle, same distance, same background. Inconsistency looks unprofessional and raises doubts.

3. **Label each image.** Treatment name, number of units or syringes, time between photos. "Lip filler, 1 syringe Juvederm, 2 weeks post-treatment."

4. **Show diversity.** Different ages, skin tones, face shapes. Patients want to see someone who looks like them.

5. **Place above the fold or immediately below.** Do not bury the gallery on a subpage three clicks deep. The gallery is the conversion engine.

6. **Slider format.** Before on the left, after on the right. Draggable slider preferred over side-by-side static images. Interactive elements increase time on page.

7. **Include a CTA after every 3-4 image pairs.** "Want results like these? Book your consultation." Do not make them scroll through 40 photos without a path to convert.

## Where Med Spas Diverge from Generic Service Businesses

### 1. Visual proof is non-negotiable.
A plumber can get away with testimonials alone. A med spa cannot. Before/after photos are the single most important conversion element. Without them, the site is dead on arrival. Before/after photos placed above the fold dramatically outperform stock photography.

### 2. Provider identity matters more than brand identity.
Patients choose the injector, not the practice. Provider bios, credentials, and individual before/after galleries outperform brand-level marketing. The site must spotlight the people, not just the logo.

### 3. Mobile-first is not a preference, it is the reality.
Over 70% of med spa traffic comes from mobile. The site must be designed for a phone screen first and adapted to desktop second. Booking must be possible in under 60 seconds on a phone.

### 4. Price sensitivity is nuanced.
Unlike home services where cheapest often wins, med spa patients associate low prices with low quality. The site must communicate value without competing on price. "Affordable" can backfire. "Investment in yourself" performs better.

### 5. Emotional stakes are higher.
A bad HVAC job means a hot house. A bad Botox job means a frozen face in every meeting for four months. The emotional risk is personal and visible. Every element of the site must reduce anxiety, not just build excitement.

### 6. The decision cycle is longer.
Home service customers call when the pipe bursts. Med spa patients research for weeks or months before booking. The site must serve both the ready-to-book visitor and the still-researching visitor with different conversion paths.

### 7. Privacy is a conversion factor.
Many patients do not want anyone to know they are getting work done. Discreet billing, private entrances, confidential consultations. If the site communicates discretion, it removes a barrier that generic service sites never face.

### 8. Compliance adds friction.
Medical advertising rules, before/after photo consent, HIPAA considerations. The site must be built with these constraints in mind. Claims must be defensible. Results language must include appropriate qualifiers.
