# Med Spa Trust Signals

Priority-ranked trust signals, niche certifications, press expectations, and before/after photo standards.

---

## Top 5 Trust Signals in Priority Order

### 1. Before/After Photo Gallery (Treatment-Specific)

**Justification:** This is the single highest-impact trust signal for med spa websites. Patients need visual proof that the provider can deliver the results they want. Every forum thread, every review site, every Reddit post about choosing a med spa mentions before/after photos. Sites that place galleries above the fold or immediately below the hero outperform those that bury them on a subpage. Before/after photos placed above the fold dramatically outperform stock photography. This is not optional. This is the conversion engine.

**Implementation:** Organize by treatment type. Use consistent lighting, angles, and backgrounds. Label with treatment details (product used, units/syringes, time elapsed). Include a CTA after every 3-4 image pairs. Show diversity in age, skin tone, and face shape.

**Without this, nothing else matters.** A med spa site without before/after photos is like a restaurant with no menu. Patients will leave.

---

### 2. Provider Credentials and Identities

**Justification:** Patients choose the injector, not the practice. The fear of choosing the wrong provider is the second-highest patient fear. Displaying credentials prominently answers the question patients are asking: "Who is going to be touching my face, and are they qualified?"

**Implementation:** Individual provider pages with professional headshot, full name, credentials (MD, DO, NP, PA), board certifications, fellowship training, years of practice, number of treatments performed, Allergan Diamond/Platinum status, and a short bio written in first person. Include each provider's own before/after gallery.

**Key detail:** Differentiate between the medical director and the injecting providers. Patients want to know who holds the needle, not just who owns the practice.

---

### 3. Review Volume and Recency

**Justification:** Patients use review count as a quality proxy. A practice with 200+ Google reviews and a 4.8 rating signals high volume and consistent satisfaction. Recency matters as much as volume. A practice with 200 reviews but none in the last 60 days raises questions.

**Implementation:** Display Google star rating and review count in the site header or hero. Embed recent reviews (last 30 days) on the homepage. Link to Google Business Profile. If RealSelf reviews exist, display those separately. Use review schema markup (AggregateRating) for rich snippets in search results.

**Target metrics:** 100+ Google reviews minimum. 4.7+ star rating. At least 5 reviews in the last 30 days.

---

### 4. Certifications and Affiliations

**Justification:** Third-party validation removes the "they said it themselves" skepticism. Certification badges signal that an outside authority has verified the provider's qualifications or volume. These badges are especially powerful when patients do not know how to evaluate medical credentials on their own.

**Implementation:** Display as a horizontal badge bar immediately below the hero section. Use official logos, not text links. Most impactful certifications listed below.

---

### 5. Patient Video Testimonials

**Justification:** Video testimonials outperform text testimonials because patients can see the results, hear the emotion, and verify that the person is real. In a niche where visual results are the product, video is the natural proof format.

**Implementation:** 60-90 second videos. Patient on camera (not voiceover with stock footage). Ask about their fear before treatment, their experience during, and their feelings after. Film in the treatment room or a branded background. Include the patient's first name and the treatment they received. Place on the homepage after the before/after gallery.

## Niche Certifications and What They Mean

### Board Certifications
- **American Board of Dermatology** -- Highest credential for skin-focused aesthetic providers
- **American Board of Plastic Surgery** -- Gold standard for surgical and injection providers
- **American Board of Facial Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery** -- Specific to facial procedures
- **American Board of Cosmetic Surgery** -- Broader aesthetic focus

**Note:** Board certification requires years of residency training, passing exams, and ongoing education. It is the single credential patients recognize and trust most. If the provider is board-certified, it should appear in the hero section.

### Manufacturer Certifications
- **Allergan Diamond Provider** -- Top 1% of Allergan product purchasers nationwide. Signals high volume and deep experience with Botox, Juvederm, and CoolSculpting.
- **Allergan Platinum Provider** -- Top 3%. Still significant but less exclusive than Diamond.
- **Galderma GAIN Program** -- Certified injector for Restylane and Dysport product lines.
- **CoolSculpting Certified Provider** -- Completed manufacturer training for CoolSculpting body contouring. Matters because CoolSculpting results are highly operator-dependent.
- **Sciton Certified** -- Training on Sciton laser platforms (BBL, HALO, etc.).

### Training and Memberships
- **American Med Spa Association (AmSpa)** -- Industry association. Shows the practice takes compliance seriously.
- **International Association for Physicians in Aesthetic Medicine (IAPAM)** -- Global credentialing body.
- **Aesthetic Multispecialty Society (AMS)** -- Focuses on non-surgical aesthetics.

## Press and Media Expectations

### What patients expect to see:
- "As seen in" logo bar featuring recognizable media outlets
- Common targets: local news features, Allure, Vogue, Harper's Bazaar, RealSelf Top Doctor, local lifestyle magazines
- Provider quoted in articles about aesthetic trends

### How to get there:
- Local TV health segments are the easiest entry point. Stations need expert guests regularly.
- Local lifestyle magazine "Best Of" lists. These are often pay-to-play or require nomination campaigns.
- RealSelf "Top Doctor" and "Worth It" designations. Require active profiles and patient reviews on the platform.
- HARO (Help a Reporter Out) or similar platforms for expert commentary opportunities.

### What to display:
- A horizontal logo bar with outlet logos
- Link to actual articles when possible
- "Featured in" language rather than "As seen on"
- Place below the trust bar or after the provider section

### What not to fake:
- Do not use media logos without a real feature. Patients will check.
- Do not use "as seen on" for paid advertisements disguised as features.

## Before/After Photo Standards

### Photography requirements:
- **Lighting:** Consistent across before and after. Natural or diffused studio light. No harsh shadows. No ring light in one and fluorescent in the other.
- **Angle:** Same angle for both shots. Front-facing for most treatments. 45-degree for jawline and cheek work. Profile for rhinoplasty or chin treatments.
- **Distance:** Same distance from camera. Use a fixed position marker on the floor if needed.
- **Background:** Plain, neutral background. Same color and setup for both shots.
- **Expression:** Neutral face in both photos. Smiling in the "after" but not the "before" creates a false comparison.
- **Makeup:** Minimal or none in both photos. If makeup is present in one, it must be present in both.
- **Timeframe:** Label clearly. "2 weeks post-treatment" or "6 weeks post-treatment." Patients need to know what to expect at different stages.

### Consent requirements:
- Written consent from every patient before any photo is displayed
- Consent should specify where photos can be used (website, social media, print)
- Many practices use a consent form at intake that covers photo usage
- Display a notice near the gallery: "All photos are of real patients who provided written consent. Individual results may vary."

### Organization:
- By treatment type first, then by provider if the gallery is large enough
- Label each pair: treatment name, product used, units/syringes, elapsed time
- Include demographic diversity: different ages, skin tones, face shapes, genders
- Minimum gallery size to be credible: 10-15 pairs per major treatment category

### Common mistakes:
- Using filtered or edited photos. Patients notice. It destroys trust.
- Inconsistent lighting that makes the "after" look better due to photography, not treatment
- Only showing best results. A range of outcomes (good and great) is more credible than perfection
- Burying the gallery three clicks deep. If patients cannot find it in two clicks, it does not exist.
