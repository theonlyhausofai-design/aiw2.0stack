# HVAC Copywriting Contract

## Voice

Direct. Plain-spoken. Honest. The voice of a contractor who does good work and does not need to oversell it.

Write like you are talking to a neighbor. Short sentences. Common words. No jargon unless the homeowner already uses that term (see vocabulary.json).

## Tone

Calm authority. The homeowner is stressed. Their AC died in July or their furnace quit in January. They are worried about cost, worried about being taken advantage of, and worried about how long they will be uncomfortable.

Meet that anxiety with steady confidence. Not hype. Not urgency tricks. Just clear, honest communication that says: we know what we are doing, we will be there fast, and we will tell you exactly what it costs before we start.

## Core Fears to Address

1. Getting ripped off. Homeowners fear inflated quotes and unnecessary repairs. Counter with upfront pricing, written estimates, and no-surprise guarantees.
2. Incompetent technicians. Counter with NATE certification, years of experience, and real reviews from real people.
3. Long wait times. Counter with same-day service promises, 24/7 availability, and specific response time commitments.
4. Hidden fees. Counter with transparent pricing language on every service page and in every CTA context.

## Writing Rules

- Lead with the benefit to the homeowner, not the feature of the company.
- Bad: "We use state-of-the-art diagnostic equipment."
- Good: "We find the problem fast so you are not paying for guesswork."
- Use "you" and "your" more than "we" and "our."
- Every heading should answer a question the homeowner is already asking.
- Every service page needs a clear price signal, even if it is a range or "starting at" figure.
- Never use passive voice for calls to action. "Call us now" not "A call can be placed."
- Never use em-dashes.
- Never use emojis.

## Emergency Copy

Emergency pages and CTAs get their own tone: slightly more urgent, but still honest. No fake countdown timers. No "ONLY 2 SLOTS LEFT" pressure. Just: "We answer 24/7. Call now and a technician will be on the way."

## Financing Copy

Financing sections should normalize the cost of HVAC systems. A new AC unit is a major purchase. Acknowledge that. Then present monthly payment options clearly. No fine print games.

## Review and Testimonial Copy

When pulling or paraphrasing reviews, keep the homeowner's original language. Do not polish it into marketing speak. "They showed up on time and fixed it the same day" is better than any copywriter's version.

## Headline Formula

For service pages, use this structure:

[City] + [Service] + [Trust Signal or Benefit]

Examples:
- "AC Repair in Dallas. Same-Day Service, Honest Pricing."
- "Furnace Installation in Chicago. NATE-Certified Technicians."
- "24/7 Emergency HVAC Service. We Answer When You Call."
